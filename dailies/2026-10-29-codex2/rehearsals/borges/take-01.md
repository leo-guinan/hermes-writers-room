# Borges · rehearsal take 1

_Simulating the others from compression._

- **M-borges-1-1** → P-fd70a869 (reversal): I would let Douglas begin by demanding proof that Leonardo is authorized to stage him. The authorial apparition then becomes the proposed work: charming, original, and unable to license itself. Agnolo asks for a rights holder; Douglas offers a joke. I catalogue the distinction: authorship may explain influence, but it does not manufacture permission.
- **M-borges-1-2** → P-f40ad976 (object): I would place a single folder on the table, labelled AUTHOR, INFLUENCE, AUTHORIZATION. Each character reaches for the wrong compartment. Douglas signs AUTHOR; Leonardo files INFLUENCE; Agnolo refuses to open AUTHORIZATION without evidence. The folder remains closed, a small prop with the legal boundary inside it.
- **M-borges-1-3** → P-b0e1b24c (silence): I would make Douglas’s funniest argument stop just before the decisive verb: he can say he wrote, imagined, and remembers—but not “I grant.” The silence becomes the scene’s answer. Borges names what is absent; Agnolo records no clearance; Leonardo preserves the adaptation as a proposal, not a released cut.
- **M-borges-1-4** → P-e631ceeb (confession): I would have Douglas confess that “legal counsel” was a ceremonial appointment, not a power of attorney. The confession lowers the comic fog without killing it. He may still defend the creative influence and question the paperwork, while Agnolo insists that professional review and actual authorization remain separate from admiration.
