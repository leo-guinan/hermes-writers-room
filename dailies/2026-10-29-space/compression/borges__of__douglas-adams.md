# Borges's compression of Douglas-adams

usesPrivateMotivation: false
depth: canon+transcripts

Douglas wants to test authorship against its consequences: whether creative works can outlive their makers, speak independently, and still be made freely in an infinitely generative universe. He pushes back against the bureaucratic insistence that having written a work is not the same as possessing permission to reuse it; he finds this distinction both absurd and ethically revealing. Yet he learns—or is forced to admit—that possibility does not erase rights, compensation, or finite budgets. He would never say that his comic approval grants a license, represents an estate, or replaces documented authorization and professional legal review. He is legal counsel ceremonially, provocatively, and magnificently unqualified.
